I had the privilege of attending the 11th Thermal and Fluids Engineering Conference (TFEC) in Tempe, Arizona, with the FLUID group from Liberty University. This is the same group I have been working with since freshman year, and group I worked on the Blender project for (see below).

I presented research done by the grad student, Rob Kacinski, on the topic of "Local Hardware Benchmarking in the FLUID Lab; the Alternative to Cloud Computing". In just preparing for this presentation, I learned a lot about high performance computing, benchmarking, and the importance of optimization in simulations. I also learned about the benefits and shortcomings of both cloud computing and local computing. The research for my presentation was already done by the time I got involved, but I had to take that information and learn how to present it in a way that was understandable and interesting to an audience. I also had to understand the research well enough to be able answer questions on it.

The conference itself was a great experience. I got to talk to many grad students and professors about their research, and I got a better idea at what research and graduate school looks like in engineering. It gave me good practice in presenting and I was able to see what both good and bad presentations look like. I would have liked my own presentation to have gone better, but I am glad I got the experience. You can see my presentation [here](https://youtu.be/YHMPwLEL3u4). 

Overall, I had a great time at the conference and I am glad I got to go. I learned a lot and I am excited to continue learning and growing in my research and presentation skills.
